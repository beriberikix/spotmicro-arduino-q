## Tools

| Tool | Used for |
| ---- | -------- |
| Hex keys or drivers, 1.5 mm (M2) and 2.5 mm (M3) | Every screw. A driver with a ball end helps in the shoulder blocks. |
| Small Phillips screwdriver | Servo horn screws, if your horns came with them |
| Needle-nose pliers or tweezers | Holding nuts and placing them in their pockets |
| Flush cutters, deburring tool or craft knife | Cleaning up prints |
| M3 tap or an old M3 screw | Chasing tight holes (optional) |
| Soldering iron, solder, flux | Servo shield headers, SZBK07 and power leads |
| Multimeter | Setting the SZBK07 to 6.0 V and checking polarity. **Required.** |
| Computer with [Arduino App Lab](https://docs.arduino.cc/software/app-lab/) | The servo tool sketch |
| USB-C cable and USB-C PD power supply (5 V 3 A) | Powering the UNO Q on the bench |
| Lab power supply (7–12 V) or a charged 2S LiPo with XT60 | Powering the servo rail on the bench |
| LiPo charger with balance lead | Charging the battery |

## Supplies

- **Small bags, egg cartons or a parts tray** for [sorting the hardware](../hardware/) by step.
- **Masking tape and a fine marker** for labelling servo plugs and printed parts.
- **Blu-Tack or a dab of grease** to keep nuts in their pockets while you close parts up.
- **Heat shrink, 16 AWG and 22–26 AWG wire, XT60 pair, Dupont jumpers.**
- **Blue (removable) threadlocker**, optional, only for metal-to-metal screws such as servo horn screws. Never use it on
  screws into plastic. Blue, not red, so you can take the robot apart again.

## Not needed

- **Glue.** Every joint is screwed and every nut sits in a printed pocket.
- **Heat-set inserts.** All threads are M2/M3 nuts in captive pockets.
- **Supports or a drill.** All parts print without supports.
