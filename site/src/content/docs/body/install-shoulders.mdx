Each pair of legs (front, rear) hangs between an **outer shoulder plate**, which holds the two shoulder servo horns,
and the **inner shoulder** in the frame, which holds the bearings for the shoulder pins.

<Parts bag="E" items={[
	[2, 'Outer shoulders'],
	[4, '25T six-arm servo horns'],
	[24, '<kbd class="hw">M2×8</kbd> with M2 nuts threaded on', 'Horns to the outer shoulders'],
	[4, '<kbd class="hw">M3×8</kbd>', 'Outer shoulders to the shoulder servo shafts'],
	[4, '625ZZ bearings', 'On the shoulder pins'],
	[8, '<kbd class="hw">M3×8</kbd> + 8 M3 nuts', 'Outer shoulders to the blocks'],

]} tools={['1.5 mm hex', '2.5 mm hex', 'Bench rig with servo_tool']} />

If you printed the MakerWorld profile, the two outer shoulders are on **Plate 1**, *Chassis plates and rails*: they're
the ones with **star-shaped recesses** for servo horns (the inner shoulders you used on the frame have round bearing
seats). The horns and screws are in bag E.

## 1. Horns onto the outer shoulders

<Render id="outer-shoulder-horns" caption="Two horns onto an outer shoulder plate" />

<Steps>

1. Lay a horn in each star-shaped recess, **hub facing out of the recess** (towards the servo), as on the knee and hip.
2. Screw each horn on with six <Hw>M2×8</Hw> (with nuts threaded on) through the middle hole of each arm.

</Steps>

Which way up the plate goes: of its four plain screw holes, the **two close together go at the bottom** (they meet the
small bottom block) and the two far apart at the top.

## 2. Leg pairs onto the outer shoulders

Each outer shoulder joins the two legs at one end of the robot. All four legs are finished, so read their tags:

<LegPick joint="shoulder" pairs={{ front: 'Front pair: one outer shoulder, channels 0 and 3', rear: 'Rear pair: the other outer shoulder, channels 6 and 9' }} />

Plug each leg's shoulder servo lead (`xx-S`) into the channel shown.

Both outer shoulders are the same part, so either one can take either pair. The tags decide which leg goes on which
side: each leg's shoulder servo shaft points out of the robot's end, so the legs lie **behind** the outer shoulder, and
its horns face back at them. Stand in front of the plate's outer face, the way you'd look at the robot from in front
(front pair) or from behind (rear pair):

- **Front pair, seen from in front:** FL on your **right**, FR on your **left** (the robot's left is on your right).
- **Rear pair, seen from behind:** RL on your **left**, RR on your **right**.

In both cases each leg's upper leg and knee are on the **outside**, away from the other leg.

<Render id="legs-to-outer" caption="Front pair (FL and FR) onto the front outer shoulder" />

<Steps>

1. Plug both front shoulder servos into the rig (channels 0 and 3) and set them to **90°**: servo_tool's **Install
   shoulders** tab (**Front pair → Move both to 90°**), or `a 0` and `a 3` in the Serial Monitor.
2. Hold the outer shoulder upright, the way it sits on the robot: horns towards the legs, the two close-together holes
   at the bottom. Push its horns onto both shoulder servo shafts so each leg hangs **straight down**, parallel to the
   plate's short edges.
3. If a leg leans to one side, pull the plate off and re-seat that leg one tooth over. If no tooth gets it straight,
   unscrew that horn, turn it one arm round in its recess and try again: that shifts the choice of positions by a
   few degrees.
4. Screw each shaft to its horn with an <Hw>M3×8</Hw> through the horn centre.
5. Press **Done: servos off** (or `o`), unplug, and repeat for the rear legs (channels 6 and 9).

</Steps>

## 3. Bearings onto the shoulder pins

<Render id="shoulder-bearings" caption="A 625ZZ bearing onto each shoulder pin of the front pair" />

Each shoulder servo mount has a bearing pin on its back wall, pointing towards the body (you fitted it on the
[hip joint](../../shoulders/hip/#2-bearing-pin-onto-the-servo-mount) page). The 625ZZ bearings go onto those pins,
and in the next step they drop into the round seats in the inner shoulder.

<Steps>

1. Push a 625ZZ bearing onto the pin of each leg in the pair, all the way down to the servo mount.
2. Do the same for the other pair: four bearings in all.

</Steps>

On servo mounts printed before 21 November 2026, the pin's M3 screw head is wider than the bearing's bore. Take the
screw out, put the bearing on, and screw it back in so its head clamps the bearing's inner ring.

## 4. Pairs onto the frame

<Render id="install-shoulders" caption="Front and rear leg pairs onto the frame" />

<Steps>

1. Drop two M3 nuts into each shoulder block at the frame's front end: into the two **rectangular slots at the block's
   outer end** (the end away from the inner shoulder), upright, as you did at the other end.
2. Slide the front leg pair onto the frame so the bearings drop into their seats in the inner shoulder and the outer
   shoulder lies flat against the ends of the blocks. The bearings may sit a little short of the bottom of their seats:
   that's the clearance, and it's fine. If instead the outer shoulder stands off the blocks, check the blocks are
   the 49.5 mm ones (see the [Frame](../frame/) page). Screw it on with four <Hw>M3×8</Hw> into those nuts: two at the top,
   two at the bottom.
3. Repeat for the rear pair at the rear end.
4. Lead all twelve servo cables up into the body, keeping each leg's three together. They plug in later.

</Steps>

<Aside type="tip">
Stand the robot on the [helper stand](../../prepare/stand/) (optional, but recommended) from here on, so the legs
hang free. A robot resting on folded legs isn't level, and the IMU levelling and servo calibration both need it level.
</Aside>
