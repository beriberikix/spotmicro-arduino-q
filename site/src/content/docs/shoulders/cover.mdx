Without it, the upper leg hangs on the hip servo's shaft alone, which wobbles. The shoulder cover bridges over the top of the hip and holds a second bearing on the outside of the upper leg, so the leg is
supported on both sides.

<Parts per="per leg" total={4} bag="D" items={[
	[1, 'Shoulder cover', 'Symmetric: the same part on every leg'],
	[1, '625ZZ bearing'],
	[3, '<kbd class="hw">M3×20</kbd> + 3 M3 nuts'],
]} tools={['2.5 mm hex']} />

The cover is on **Plate 7, Upper leg bottom shells and shoulder covers** (the four curved parts); the bearing and
screws are the last of bag D.

<Render id="shoulder-cover" caption="Shoulder cover over the hip, onto the cap extension and the bearing pin" />

<Steps>

1. Drop a 625ZZ into the cover's bearing hole **from the inside** (the side that faces the upper leg). It's a slip fit, not a press
   fit: the hole has a lip on the outside face that stops it.
2. Slide the cover's bearing over the M3 bearing pin on the upper leg's top shell, and bring its other end against the
   extended servo cap.
3. Drop an M3 nut into each of the three slots in the cover. Bolt the cover to the cap with three <Hw>M3×20</Hw>
   from the cap side: the heads sit in the cap's recesses and thread into the nuts in the cover.
4. Check for play: hold the shoulder joint and try to wiggle the upper leg sideways. It should feel solid now. Don't
   swing the leg round by hand, though: that back-drives the hip servo.

</Steps>

<Aside type="note" title="Top shells printed before 21 November 2026">
On older top shells the pin is held by an M3×8, whose head is wider than the bearing's bore, so the cover won't go
over it. Take the screw out, slide the 625ZZ onto the pin, put the screw back (its head now clamps the bearing's inner
ring), and lower the cover over the bearing.
</Aside>
