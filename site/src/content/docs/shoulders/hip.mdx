The **shoulder joint** is the extended servo mount plus the extended servo cap. The cap carries the horn that the
hip servo drives, and its extension is where the shoulder cover will bolt on.

Bag D also holds the parts for the next two pages: the shoulder servo's four <Hw>M3×20</Hw>, nuts and dampeners, and
the shoulder cover's 625ZZ bearing and three <Hw>M3×20</Hw> with nuts. Leave those in the bag.

Per leg you need one servo mount, one servo cap and one bearing pin. If you printed the MakerWorld profile, all three
are on **Plate 3, Shoulder joints and head mounts**. Take a pin with the **M3 bore**, the same kind as on the upper
leg's top shell: an M3 screw goes through it, while the M2 ones are too small.

## Which servo mount?

Both shoulder servos on a side face **outwards**: the front one towards the nose, the rear one towards the tail. So each
side uses **one normal and one mirrored** mount: FL and RR take the same version, FR and RL the other. Mounts printed
from the current files have **FL RR** or **FR RL** engraved on a face that ends up hidden.

## 1. Horn onto the extended cap

<Render id="hip-horn-cap" caption="Horn onto the extended servo cap" />

<Steps>

1. If your M2×8 don't have nuts on them yet, screw an M2 nut all the way onto six of them. The nut is a spacer: a
   bare M2×8 pokes through and rubs on the servo. (M2×6 screws without nuts work too.)
2. Screw the horn onto the cap through the middle hole of each arm, as on the knee. The horn's **hub goes down into
   the cap's star recess**, so it pokes through the cap's centre hole, ready to go onto the servo shaft.

</Steps>

## 2. Bearing pin onto the servo mount

<Render id="shoulder-pin" caption="Bearing pin on the back of the shoulder servo mount" />

<Steps>

1. Press an M3 nut into the hex pocket on the inside of the servo mount's back wall.
2. From the outside, put an <Hw>M3×8</Hw> through the bearing pin and the hole, and screw it into the nut. This pin will turn in the bearing in the inner shoulder plate.

</Steps>

## 3. Cap onto the servo mount

<Render id="cap-to-mount" caption="Extended cap with horn onto the shoulder servo mount" />

<Steps>

1. Put four M3 nuts into the pockets in the base of the servo mount.
2. Hold the cap against the mount's base, with the **horn's hub facing away from the mount** (it goes onto the hip
   servo in step 4) and the cap's **extension** (the end with the slot and three small holes, where the shoulder cover
   bolts on later) at the **same end as the mount's servo cable slot** (the slot in the top wall that the servo lead comes out of). The four screw holes are square, so the cap fits
   four ways round: only this one is right. The cap is the same for every leg.
3. Screw the cap on with four <Hw>M3×8</Hw>.

</Steps>

## 4. Shoulder joint onto the hip servo

<Render id="shoulder-to-leg" caption="Shoulder joint onto the upper leg's hip servo" />

<Steps>

1. Open servo_tool's web page (**Hip joint** tab) and pick the leg. Plug the hip servo's lead into the channel it shows
   and press **Move**: it goes to the angle for the upper leg **hanging straight down**.
2. Hold the shoulder joint so the upper leg hangs straight down, **parallel to the servo mount's side walls**, cable
   slot end up. Push the cap's horn onto the hip servo shaft in the position nearest to parallel. If none is, pull it
   off, turn the horn one arm round in the cap and try again. Don't screw it yet.
3. Press **Check it**. The servo turns 60°, one horn arm, to the standing angle (120° for left legs, 60° for right
   legs). Hold the servo mount still, cable slot end up: the upper leg should swing up **towards the side with the
   bearing pin on front legs, away from it on rear legs**, ending about a third of the way up from straight down
   (picture below). If it swings the other way, press **No**: the page gives you the other straight-down angle to fit
   it at again. It should pass first time (60° straight down on the left legs, 120° on the right legs, on the first build), and the page remembers it for the other leg on that side.
4. Screw it on with an <Hw>M3×8</Hw> through the horn centre, and press **Done** to switch the servo off.

</Steps>

Without the web page, use the Serial Monitor: `s <ch> 60` on left legs or `s <ch> 120` on right legs for straight
down, `a <ch>` for the check; if the leg swung the wrong way, refit it at `s <ch> 180` (left) or `s <ch> 0` (right).
