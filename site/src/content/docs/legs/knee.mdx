The two knee plates straddle the lower leg: the **servohorn mount** grips the knee servo's shaft on the inner side, and
the **ball-bearing mount** rides on the bearing pin on the outer side. The upper leg is bolted between them on the next
page.

If you printed the MakerWorld profile, both knee plates come from **Plate 4, Upper leg frames**: four servo-horn mounts
and four ball-bearing mounts, half of each mirrored. The horn, screws and bearing are in bag B.

<Aside type="note" title="The renders show the standing pose">
The CAD model stands the robot up, so the renders show the knee bent. You assemble it **folded flat**, as described
below.
</Aside>

## Which one?

Parts printed from this repository's current files have their side engraved on a face that ends up hidden: **L** or **R**, and **FL RR** or **FR RL** on the shoulder servo mount. For parts without it, put each one down as described and pick your leg: the green check marks the version that leg takes.

## Match the plates to each lower leg

## 1. Horn onto the servohorn mount

<Render id="knee-horn" caption="Six-arm horn onto the servohorn mount" />

<Steps>

1. Thread an M2 nut all the way onto each <Hw>M2×8</Hw> (if you didn't when sorting). The nut is a spacer: a bare M2×8
   would poke through and rub on the servo.
2. Lay the horn in the star-shaped recess on the mount's inner face, with its **hub towards the servo** (the
   splined hole faces away from the plate, ready to go onto the shaft).
3. Screw it on through the **middle hole of each arm** into the mount.

</Steps>

## 2. Mount onto the knee servo

<Render id="knee-horn-plate" caption="Servohorn mount onto the knee servo's shaft" />

<Steps>

1. Plug the lower leg's extension lead (the end you tagged `xx-K`) into its channel on the bench rig and send `a <ch>`: **180° for left legs, 0° for right legs**
   (channels 2, 5, 8, 11; see the [angle table](../../prepare/servos/#assembly-angles)). Leave it powered.
2. Hold the servohorn mount against the knee, **folded back over the lower leg**: the round end goes on the shaft and
   the long part points **towards the foot**, lying along the wrist, with the square end (two screw holes) bending
   out past the wrist's curved edge. If the mount sticks out past the end of the leg, away from the foot, it's on
   backwards.
3. Push the horn onto the shaft. One arm of the horn should point **exactly perpendicular** to the wrist's straight
   edge.
   The horn has 25 teeth, so it isn't symmetrical: if the plate sits a little off, pull it off, unscrew the horn from
   the mount, turn the horn to a different arm, and try again until it lines up.
4. Screw the mount to the shaft with an <Hw>M3×8</Hw> through the centre of the horn.
5. Send `o <ch>` and unplug the servo.

</Steps>

<Aside type="caution">
Getting this right matters. If the plate is a tooth off, the leg either won't fold flat at rest, or the servo will
push against the stop at 0°/180° and stutter, drawing current all the time. If the servo can't reach a
reference pose later, refit the horn one tooth over.
</Aside>

## 3. Ball-bearing mount

<Render id="knee-bearing-plate" caption="Bearing into the ball-bearing mount, then onto the pin" />

<Steps>

1. Push a 625ZZ bearing into the ball-bearing mount from the **flat face** (the one that was on the print bed) until it
   stops against the lip inside the bore. It stays about **2 mm proud** of the flat face. Depending on your printer it
   can be a loose fit and fall out on its own: that's fine, the pin and the upper leg hold it in place.
2. Push the mount onto the bearing pin on the outer side of the lower leg, lined up with the servohorn mount on the
   other side.

</Steps>
