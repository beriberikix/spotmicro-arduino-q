The upper leg is two shells around the **hip servo**. The top shell has an extra hole for the bearing pin that the
shoulder cover will ride on. Both servo leads, the knee's and the hip's, leave through the hole in the top shell, and later go on through the slot
in the servo cap under the shoulder cover.

## Which one?

Parts printed from this repository's current files have their side engraved on a face that ends up hidden: **L** or **R**, and **FL RR** or **FR RL** on the shoulder servo mount. For parts without it, put each one down as described and pick your leg: the green check marks the version that leg takes.

## Match the hip servo and shells to each leg

Work on one leg at a time. Take a finished lower leg, read its tag, and pick the **hip** servo (`xx-H`) and the shells
of the matching version:

<LegPick joint="hip" parts={[{ file: 'bottom-shell', name: 'Bottom shell' }, { file: 'top-shell', name: 'Top shell' }]} />

## 1. Hip servo into the bottom shell

<Render id="upper-servo" caption="Hip servo and nuts into the bottom shell" />

<Steps>

1. Fit the four rubber dampeners to the hip servo.
2. Put four M3 nuts in the holes by the servo pocket.
3. Push the servo into its pocket. The two inner nuts are now held by the servo; the two outer ones can still fall out,
   so be careful.

</Steps>

## 2. Shoulder-cover pin on the top shell

<Aside type="caution" title="Do this before you close the shells">
The pin's nut sits inside the top shell. Once the shells are screwed together you can't reach it, and the only way
back is taking the upper leg apart.
</Aside>

The top shell has a small hole in its long side wall, near the rounded end (away from the knee). On the inside,
that hole sits in a **hexagonal nut pocket**. The bearing pin goes on the outside, and later the shoulder cover's bearing rides on it.

<Steps>

1. Drop an M2 nut into the hex pocket on the inside of the top shell, all the way to the bottom.
2. From the outside, put an <Hw>M2×8</Hw> through the bearing pin and the hole, and screw it into the nut.
3. Tighten it so the pin sits square and doesn't wobble.

</Steps>

<Aside type="note" title="Top shells printed before 21 November 2026">
Older top shells have an M3 hole and nut pocket here. Use an M3×8 and M3 nut with the M3-bore pin
(<code>Shoulder_Joint_Ball_Bearing_Pin_M3x8</code>), and on the [Shoulder cover](../../shoulders/cover/) page put the
bearing on the pin before the screw: the M3 head is wider than the bearing's bore.
</Aside>

## 3. Route the cables and close the shells

<Render id="upper-shells" caption="Top shell onto the bottom shell, four M3×20 through the servo" />

This is the fiddly part: there is very little room between the shells.

<figure>
	<img src={`${import.meta.env.BASE_URL.replace(/\/$/, '')}/renders/upper-leg-cable.png`} alt="The upper leg's top shell on its own, from the side that meets the bottom shell, knee end on the left and hip end on the right. A red dashed line runs from the knee end, where the knee cable enters between the shells, along the groove beside the shell's edge, to the rounded hole in the hip end, where it leaves together with the hip servo's lead." width="1040" height="660" loading="lazy" style="background:#fff;border-radius:0.4rem" />
	<figcaption>The knee cable's route through the top shell: in between the shells at the knee end, along the groove, and out of the hole at the hip end with the hip lead.</figcaption>
</figure>

<Steps>

1. Feed the **knee servo's extension cable** (from the lower leg) through the **front hole** of the top shell.
2. Feed the **hip servo's lead** through the same hole.
3. Bring the shells together while gently pulling both cables through the hole. The knee cable goes into the shells
   through the gap between them, and runs in the **groove under the top shell**, which is made for it.
4. With the shells closed, check the cables still slide back and forth through the hole.
5. Screw the servo and both shells together with four <Hw>M3×20</Hw>.

</Steps>

<Aside type="tip">
Avoid thick braided sleeving here: there isn't room. Bare servo cable works; if you must sleeve it, use
the thinnest you can find.
</Aside>


## 4. Bolt the shells between the knee plates

<Render id="upper-to-knee" caption="Upper leg between the servohorn mount and the ball-bearing mount" />

<Steps>

1. Put four M3 nuts into the four rear-facing pockets of the shells.
2. Slide the knee end of the shells between the two knee plates, so the holes in the plates line up with the holes in
   the shells.
3. Screw everything together with four <Hw>M3×20</Hw>.

</Steps>

The leg should now fold flat: that's the robot's resting position. Turn the knee gently by hand, servo
unpowered: it should move without binding against the shells.
