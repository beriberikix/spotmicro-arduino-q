Power comes from a 2S LiPo (6.6–8.4 V) and splits in two after the main switch:

- **Servos:** the SZBK07 drops it to 6.0 V for the shield's V+ terminal. It holds 6.0 V down to about 7.5 V of battery,
  then follows the battery down, still within the servos' rating.
- **Brains:** the battery goes straight into the UNO Q's **VIN** (7–24 V), which also powers the Media Carrier and
  cameras. The GY-521 and the shield's logic run from the UNO Q's 3.3 V.

<PowerDiagram mode="robot" />

<Steps>

1. Solder the female XT60 to a 16 AWG lead for the battery side. The battery lead runs **under the plate**, beside the
   battery, and up through the **slot at the rear-left corner**.
2. Wire the switch in the positive lead.
3. From the switch, run 16 AWG to the SZBK07's **IN+**, and the ground to **IN−**.
4. From the switch output, run a thinner lead (22 AWG) to the UNO Q's **VIN**, for example the VIN pin on the shield's
   power header, which passes straight through to the UNO Q. Its ground goes to GND on the same header.
5. Keep the SZBK07 → shield V+ lead from the bench rig, shortened to length.
6. Before plugging in the battery: meter **between VIN and GND**, and **between the SZBK07 input terminals**, to
   check there's no short and the polarity is right.

</Steps>

<Aside type="caution" title="Open question: the main switch">
The BOM's 19 mm latching push button fits the covers' cutout, but these buttons are usually rated for 3–5 A, and the
servo rail can draw several amps while walking and more at stall. Options to decide before this step is final:
a switch rated for 15 A or more, switching only the UNO Q and keeping the XT60 as the servo disconnect, or the button
driving a MOSFET or relay (see [ideas.md](https://github.com/beriberikix/spotmicro-arduino-q/blob/main/docs/ideas.md#power)).
</Aside>

<Aside type="tip">
Below about 3.5 V per cell (7.0 V for the pack) the UNO Q's VIN drops below its minimum. Stop and charge at that point;
it also protects the LiPo.
</Aside>
