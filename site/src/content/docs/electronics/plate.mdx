## 1. IMU

The IMU sits on four posts in the strip between the board stack and the right side rail, chip side up, with its two
STEMMA QT sockets facing front and rear. On this page "front" is the robot's front: the end of the plate with the
SZBK07's four tall posts. The board stack's pads are at the rear.

The IMU's two sockets are identical and wired together, so either one works. What matters is which way it points:

<Steps>

1. Turn the IMU so the **Y arrow** printed above the chip (`X↓ Y→`) points to the front of the plate. Its X arrow then
   points to the robot's right, and every robot's IMU is fitted the same way. Plug one end of the 200 mm STEMMA QT
   cable into the socket that now faces the front.
2. Screw the IMU onto its four posts with four <Hw>M2×5</Hw> self-tapping screws. They cut their own thread in the
   pilot holes: snug, not tight.

</Steps>

The cable's other end goes into the UNO Q in step 3.

<Aside type="note">
The UNO Q's Qwiic socket is its second I2C bus (`Wire1`), separate from the shield's (`Wire`). Some early UNO Q
software releases had trouble with `Wire1`. If the IMU doesn't show up on it, cut the cable and solder its four wires
to the shield's 3V, GND, SDA and SCL instead: the IMU then shares the shield's bus (address 0x68, no clash with 0x40).
</Aside>

## 2. Camera cables and VIN leads onto the carrier

Do this before the carrier goes in: the connectors are on the carrier, the cables run forwards **under** the SZBK07,
and once the UNO Q and shield are on, there's no way to reach VIN.

<Steps>

1. Turn the carrier over. The two camera connectors sit side by side along one long edge of its underside, labelled
   **CAMERA0** and **CAMERA1**. (The single connector on the opposite edge is **DISPLAY**: leave it empty.)
2. Open each camera connector's latch, push in the **Mini** end of a 200 mm cable (contacts facing the right way, see
   the carrier's marking) and close the latch.
3. **CAMERA0** goes to the **right** camera: mark its cable `R`. **CAMERA1** goes to the left camera.
4. Find the row of four through-hole pads labelled **3V3, 5V, VIN, GND** at the carrier's corner by the angled edge,
   on the same long edge as the camera connectors. The UNO Q's own VIN ends up buried under the shield, so the battery
   feeds it through the carrier's **VIN** pad instead, which passes through to the UNO Q.
5. Solder the **30 cm red** 22 AWG wire into **VIN** and the **15 cm black** one into **GND**, from the underside,
   so nothing stands up into the 3 mm gap under the UNO Q. Keep the joints low, lay both wires flat to the board's
   short edge by the angled corners, and tag the free ends `VIN` and `GND`.
6. Meter: VIN and GND not shorted to each other, or to 5V and 3V3.

</Steps>

The free ends get connected on the [Power wiring](../power/) page: VIN to the push button, GND to the SZBK07.

## 3. Carrier, UNO Q and shield

<Render id="plate-carrier" caption="Carrier onto its pads, clamped by four M3×8 screws and washers" start="exploded" />

The carrier has no mounting holes, so four screws clamp its two short edges instead: each goes into a short post beside
the board, and its washer overlaps the board's edge by about 1 mm.

<Steps>

1. Lay the carrier on its four pads, camera connectors down and at the front (towards the SZBK07 posts), just behind
   the two small **front stops**, with the VIN and GND leads out past its left end. **CAMERA0** is then on the right, and the board's angled corners point to the left
   side rail.
2. Put an M3 washer on each of four <Hw>M3×8</Hw> and screw them into the four clamp posts, two beside each short edge
   of the carrier. They cut their own thread in the pilot holes. Tighten until the washers just grip the board: snug,
   not tight, or the washers dig into it.

</Steps>

<Render id="plate-unoq" caption="UNO Q onto the carrier's bottom connectors" start="exploded" />

<Steps>

3. Press the UNO Q onto the carrier's two long connectors, its outline over the carrier's: angled corners to the
   left, USB-C to the right. Push over the pads, not in the middle of the board.
4. Plug the IMU cable into the UNO Q's **Qwiic socket**, on the board's left edge, now: the shield covers it once it's
   on. Run the cable forwards along the left side, across the plate in front of the board stack and the front stops,
   and back to the IMU. It crosses **over** the two camera cables in the gap between the front stops and the SZBK07
   posts; keep it there, not under the SZBK07, where the clearance belongs to the camera cables. If it ends up in the
   wrong place later, unplug it at the IMU and feed it through: nothing needs unscrewing.

</Steps>

<Render id="plate-shield" caption="Servo shield onto the UNO Q, servo plugs forwards" start="exploded" />

<Steps>

5. Press the servo shield onto the UNO Q, servo plugs pointing forwards. The IMU cable passes under the servo plugs.

</Steps>

No USB-C cable stays in the robot: the UNO Q runs from the battery through VIN (see [Power](../power/)), and you program it over
Wi-Fi, which you set up on the bench. If you ever need it wired, for example to reflash it, take the top cover off and
plug in for that session only.

## 4. SZBK07

<Render id="camera-cables" caption="Camera cables flat along the plate, out through the front shoulder plates" start="exploded" />

<Steps>

1. Lay the two camera cables flat along the plate towards the front and out through the front shoulder plates to the
   head. The modelled route uses about 172 mm of each 200 mm cable, leaving about 28 mm to fold flat inside the
   shoulder box.
2. Stick a strip of Kapton (or electrical) tape over the solder joints on the SZBK07's underside, above all under the
   two terminal blocks. Their leads stick out about 3.5 mm; on the 6 mm posts that leaves about 2 mm over the cables,
   and the tape keeps a sharp lead from ever rubbing through one.

</Steps>

<Render id="plate-szbk07" caption="SZBK07 onto its four posts over the camera cables, OUT end to the rear" start="exploded" />

<Steps>

3. Turn the SZBK07 so its **OUT** terminal block, with the three small capacitors and the two blue pots, faces the
   **rear**, towards the board stack: that keeps the lead to the shield's V+ short. The **IN** terminal block, with the
   two large capacitors and the small ON/OFF slide switch, faces the **front**. Seen from above, the pots end up at
   the rear-left corner.
4. Screw it onto its four posts over the cables with four <Hw>M3×8</Hw>. The screws thread into the pilot holes; no
   nuts.
5. Check the board's ON/OFF slide switch is at **ON**. It's easy to knock while handling the board, and in the OFF
   position the servos get nothing.

</Steps>
