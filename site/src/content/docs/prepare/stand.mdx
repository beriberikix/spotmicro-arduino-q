<Aside type="note" title="Optional, but recommended">
You can build the robot without the stand. From [Shoulders into the frame](../../body/install-shoulders/)
on, though, you'll want it for every servo check, the first power-on, IMU levelling and calibration. A robot resting on
its folded legs isn't level, and a leg pressed against the bench can stall a servo. It's a short print (about 2.7 h, 92 g)
and ten minutes to put together.
</Aside>

The stand is two upright A-frames, one under the front of the belly and one under the rear, joined by two braces. Each
A-frame is a **top** bolted to a **bottom**. The robot's bottom cover sits in the two cradles, held by their sides.

Print it from the MakerWorld **Helper stand** profile (two plates, one stand each) or from
[`stl/optional/helper-stand/`](https://github.com/beriberikix/spotmicro-arduino-q/tree/main/stl/optional/helper-stand).
Black PLA, 20% infill, 3 walls, no supports.

	[4, 'Stand foot'],
	[4, '<kbd class="hw">M3×12</kbd> + 4 M3 nuts', 'Top to bottom; M3×10 also fits'],

]} tools={['2.5 mm hex', 'Pliers or a 5.5 mm spanner for the nuts']} />

The top and the bottom are easy to mix up when they're lying flat:

| | Top | Bottom |
| --- | --- | --- |
| End | Curved cradle | Triangle, with a small square stub at each outer corner |
| Big holes in the post | **5** | **4** |
| Small pair of holes for a brace | Just below the cradle | Just above where the triangle starts |

The two braces are identical, and each is the same at both ends, so either brace goes either way round.

## Build each A-frame

<figure>
	<img src={`${import.meta.env.BASE_URL.replace(/\/$/, '')}/renders/stand-aframe.png`} alt="Exploded A-frame: the stand top above, the stand bottom below, their thin tab ends overlapping, two screws and nuts at the joint, and a foot under each corner of the base." width="422" height="1082" style="background:#fff;border-radius:0.4rem" />
	<figcaption>One A-frame: the tabs overlap, two M3×12 bolt them, a foot goes on each base corner.</figcaption>
</figure>

<Steps>

1. Lay a top and a bottom on the table, end to end, so their posts point at each other. The end of each post is a tab
   half as thick as the post, and both tabs are on the same side. Turn the bottom over, so its tab sits on top of the
   top's tab and the two make one post of full thickness. The two holes in each tab line up.
2. Bolt the tabs together with two <Hw>M3×12</Hw> and nuts. The holes are a loose fit for M3: put a washer under each
   head and each nut if you have them, square the post up, and tighten gently so the heads don't sink into the plastic.
3. Push a foot onto the square stub at each outer corner of the base, socket side up. It's a tight push fit; if one is
   loose, glue it.
4. Build the second A-frame the same way.

</Steps>

## Join the two A-frames

<figure>
	<img src={`${import.meta.env.BASE_URL.replace(/\/$/, '')}/renders/stand-join.png`} alt="The two A-frames standing 120 mm apart, joined by two blue braces, one just under the cradles and one just above the bases, with M3×16 screws going through the posts into nuts in the brace ends." width="776" height="1226" style="background:#fff;border-radius:0.4rem" />
	<figcaption>The braces sit between the posts; the screws go in from the outside into nuts in the brace ends.</figcaption>
</figure>

<Steps>

1. Each brace end has two holes going into it and, a few millimetres in, two slots that go right through the brace.
   Drop an M3 nut into each slot, four per brace. They should line up with the holes; push a screw in to check.
2. Stand the two A-frames up facing each other. Hold a brace end against the pair of small holes just below the cradle
   on one A-frame and screw it on from the outside with two <Hw>M3×16</Hw> through the post into the nuts. Do the same
   at the other end with the second A-frame. No washers here: these holes are a snug fit.
3. Join the pairs just above the bases with the second brace the same way.
4. Set the stand on a flat surface and check it doesn't rock. If it does, loosen the tab screws, press the stand flat,
   and tighten them again.

</Steps>
