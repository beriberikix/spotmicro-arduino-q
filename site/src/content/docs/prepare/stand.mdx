<Aside type="note" title="Optional, but recommended">
You can build the robot without the stand. From [Shoulders into the frame](../../body/install-shoulders/)
on, though, you'll want it for every servo check, the first power-on, IMU levelling and calibration. A robot resting on
its folded legs isn't level, and a leg pressed against the bench can stall a servo. It's a short print (about 2.7 h, 92 g)
and ten minutes to put together.
</Aside>

The stand is two upright A-frames, one under the front of the belly and one under the rear, joined by two braces. Each
A-frame is a **top** (the curved cradle on a post) bolted to a **bottom** (the triangular base). The robot's bottom
cover sits in the cradles, held by their sides.

Print it from the MakerWorld **Helper stand** profile (two plates, one stand each) or from
[`stl/optional/helper-stand/`](https://github.com/beriberikix/spotmicro-arduino-q/tree/main/stl/optional/helper-stand).
Black PLA, 20% infill, 3 walls, no supports.

## Build each A-frame

<Steps>

1. Lay a top and a bottom on the table so their posts point at each other. The end of each post is a tab half as thick
   as the post. Overlap the two tabs so they make one post of full thickness, with the two holes in each tab lined up.
2. Bolt the tabs together with two <Hw>M3×12</Hw>, a washer under each head and nut if you have them. The holes are a
   loose fit for M3, so square the post up before you tighten.
3. Push a foot onto each of the two outer corners of the base, the square socket over the square corner. They're a
   tight push fit. If one is loose, glue it.
4. Build the second A-frame the same way.

</Steps>

## Join the two A-frames

<Steps>

1. Each brace end has two holes going into it and, a few millimetres in, two slots across them. Drop an M3 nut into
   each slot, four per brace. They should line up with the holes; push a screw in to check.
2. Each A-frame has two pairs of small holes side by side on its post: one just below the cradle, one just above the
   point where the base splits into its triangle. Stand the two A-frames up facing each other. Hold a brace end against
   the upper pair on one A-frame and screw it on with two <Hw>M3×16</Hw> through the post into the nuts. Do the same
   at the other end with the second A-frame.
3. Join the lower pairs with the second brace the same way.
4. Set the stand on a flat surface and check it doesn't rock. If it does, loosen the tab screws, press the stand flat,
   and tighten them again.

</Steps>

<Check id="stand-hardware" title="Stand screw sizes">
No source lists the stand's hardware: the sizes here come from measuring the parts. The tab holes are 4.0 mm, which
might have been meant for M4. Check that M3×12 holds the tabs firmly and that M3×16 reaches the nuts in the braces
without bottoming out.
</Check>
