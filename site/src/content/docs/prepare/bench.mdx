Before any servo goes into a leg, you'll test it and set it to its assembly angle. That needs a small bench rig: the
UNO Q with the servo shield on top, and the SZBK07 feeding the servos 6.0 V. The same boards go into the robot later,
so the soldering here is done once.

## The shield on a 3.3 V board

The UNO Q's microcontroller runs at **3.3 V**, while the shield ships set up for 5 V Arduinos. Two changes make it safe,
both on the shield's solder side. Check the
[Adafruit guide](https://learn.adafruit.com/adafruit-16-channel-pwm-slash-servo-shield) photos if you're unsure which
pads are which.

<Steps>

1. **Logic voltage jumper (SJ1): 5V → 3V.** SJ1 is a three-pad solder jumper. Its middle pad is the shield's logic
   supply (VCC): it powers the PCA9685 and pulls the I2C lines up through two 10 kΩ resistors. From the factory the
   middle pad is joined to the **5V** pad. Remove that solder (or cut the trace) and bridge the middle pad to the
   **3V** pad instead.

   With VCC at 3.3 V the I2C lines stay at 3.3 V and the servo signal is 3.3 V, which DS3218s accept.

2. **Disconnect A4/A5 from SDA/SCL.** On classic Arduinos A4/A5 *are* the I2C pins, so the shield joins them. On the
   UNO Q they are separate microcontroller pins (A4/A5 are a second I2C bus, `Wire2`), so the shield ties two pairs of
   pins together. Adafruit's guide says to cut the traces between A4/A5 and the SDA/SCL pins next to them when using
   other boards. Cut them, and check with the meter that A4–SDA and A5–SCL no longer beep.

3. **Check with a meter.** With nothing plugged in: the shield's VCC (the middle pad of SJ1) has continuity to the 3V
   header pin and **not** to the 5V pin.

</Steps>

<Aside type="caution" title="Draft: confirm on the real board">
Adafruit doesn't document SJ1's factory setting in words. Look at your board: the blob should be on the 5V side, and
it's the one you move. Leaving A4/A5 joined to SDA/SCL is harmless as long as no sketch uses A4/A5 or `Wire2`, but
cutting them removes the risk.
</Aside>

## Solder the shield

<Steps>

1. **Header strip.** Solder the **plain male header strip** that comes with the shield (not stacking headers): the
   UNO Q's headers are female, and the Media Carrier goes underneath, not on top. Put the strips into the UNO Q, lay
   the shield on, and solder one pin at each end first, so they come out straight.
2. **Right-angle servo headers.** Solder the 3×4 right-angle headers on channels 0–11 (and 12–15 if you have a fourth),
   all pointing the same way, out over the edge of the shield where the servo headers sit. In the robot that edge faces
   the front, so the plugs point towards the SZBK07 (see [Electronics plate](../../electronics/plate/)). The
   silkscreen marks the three rows (PWM signal, V+, GND); note which row is GND, because the servo plug's brown or
   black wire goes there.
3. **Terminal block.** Solder the blue two-way terminal block (V+ and GND).
4. **Capacitor.** Solder the capacitor into C2, minding polarity: the stripe (−) goes to the GND side, as marked on the
   silkscreen. Adafruit's rule of thumb is about 100 µF per servo; 1000 µF covers twelve DS3218s at idle and in gentle
   moves.

</Steps>

## Set the SZBK07 to 6.0 V

**Do this before any servo is connected.** A full 2S pack is 8.4 V, which would burn DS3218s (rated 4.8–6.8 V). A student
team burned a servo controller's capacitor with an unchecked regulator.

<Steps>

1. Wire the bench supply (or LiPo) to the SZBK07's **IN+ / IN−** with 16 AWG. Check polarity with the meter.
2. Switch on. With nothing on the output, measure **OUT+ / OUT−** with the multimeter.
3. Turn the **voltage (CV)** potentiometer until the meter reads **6.0 V**. It can take many turns.
4. If your module has a **current limit (CC)** pot, turn it to maximum for now.
5. Switch off.

</Steps>

## Wire the bench rig

<PowerDiagram mode="bench" />

<Steps>

1. Press the shield onto the UNO Q.
2. Run 16 AWG from the SZBK07 **OUT+** to the shield's **V+** terminal, and **OUT−** to **GND**. The terminal block has
   reverse-polarity protection, but don't rely on it.
3. Connect the UNO Q to your computer with USB-C.

</Steps>

<Aside type="danger" title="Two separate supplies">
The servo rail (V+) is isolated from the UNO Q's VIN and 5V on the shield, and only the grounds are shared. Never
connect V+ to the UNO Q's 5V or VIN pins, and never take servo current from the UNO Q.
</Aside>
