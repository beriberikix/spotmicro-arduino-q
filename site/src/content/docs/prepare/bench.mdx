Before any servo goes into a leg, you'll test it and set it to its assembly angle. That needs a small bench rig: the
UNO Q with the servo shield on top, and the SZBK07 feeding the servos 6.0 V. The same boards go into the robot later,
so the soldering here is done once.

## The shield on a 3.3 V board

The UNO Q's microcontroller runs at **3.3 V**, while the shield ships set up for 5 V Arduinos. Two changes make it
safe: move one jumper, and leave two header pins out.

<Steps>

1. **VCC select: 5V → 3V.** VCC select is the three-pad jumper on the **top** of the shield, between the Reset button
   and the IOREF/3V/5V header. Its middle pad is the shield's logic supply (VCC): it powers the PCA9685 and pulls the
   I2C lines up through two 10 kΩ resistors. From the factory the middle pad is joined to the **5V** pad by a thin
   copper bridge printed on the board, with no solder on it.

   <img src={`${import.meta.env.BASE_URL.replace(/\/$/, '')}/photos/bench/vcc-select.jpg`} alt="Close-up of the VCC select jumper: three square pads labelled 3V on the left and 5V on the right. The middle and right pads are joined by a thin copper bridge; the left pad is separate." width="900" height="334" style="max-width:30rem" />

   Cut the bridge between the middle and 5V pads with a craft knife: a few firm strokes across the gap, until the
   meter shows no continuity between them. Then flow a blob of solder across the middle and **3V** pads.

   With VCC at 3.3 V the I2C lines stay at 3.3 V and the servo signal is 3.3 V, which DS3218s accept.

2. **Leave A4 and A5 out.** On classic Arduinos A4/A5 *are* the I2C pins, so the shield joins them to SDA/SCL (hence
   the SDA and SCL over pins 4 and 5). The traces run under the solder mask on the back, so they can't be cut cleanly.
   On the UNO Q, A4/A5 are separate microcontroller pins (a second I2C bus, `Wire2`), and the shield talks to the
   PCA9685 through the separate SDA/SCL pins next to AREF ("Uses only the SCL/SDA pins!"). So just don't connect A4
   and A5: when you solder the header strip below, pull those two pins out of the 6-pin analog strip with pliers
   first, or snip them flush.

3. **Check with a meter.** With nothing plugged in:
   - the shield's VCC (the middle pad) beeps to the **3V** header pin and **not** to the 5V pin;
   - once the headers are on, the UNO Q's A4 and A5 sockets don't beep to SDA and SCL.

</Steps>

## Solder the shield

<Steps>

1. **Header strip.** Solder the **plain male header strip** that comes with the shield (not stacking headers): the
   UNO Q's headers are female, and the Media Carrier goes underneath, not on top. Put the strips into the UNO Q, lay
   the shield on, and solder one pin at each end first, so they come out straight. Remember the 6-pin analog strip
   goes in without its A4 and A5 pins (step 2 above).

   **Then trim the power and analog header pins flush** on top of the shield with flush cutters, and cover that row
   of joints with a strip of Kapton (or electrical) tape. The servo plugs lie right over them. Untrimmed, the pin
   tips stand up into the bottom (GND) row of the servo headers and can short a servo's GND to 5V, VIN or an analog
   pin.
2. **Right-angle servo headers.** Solder the 3×4 right-angle headers on channels 0–11 (and 12–15 if you have a fourth),
   all pointing the same way. Each header covers one block of four channels: its four columns go into the four
   channels (0–3, 4–7, 8–11) and its three rows into PWM, V+ and GND. The pins then point **across** the rows, past
   GND and out towards the board edge with the IOREF/RST/3V/5V/GND/VIN header, not sideways along the row (that would
   cover the next block) and not inwards (C2 sits there). The plugs pass over the power header's solder joints and
   hang past the board edge: that's why the shield uses plain male headers, whose plastic is underneath, and not
   stacking headers, whose sockets would sit in the way on top. In the robot that edge faces the front, so the plugs point
   towards the SZBK07 (see [Electronics plate](../../electronics/plate/)). The
   silkscreen marks the three rows (PWM signal, V+, GND); note which row is GND, because the servo plug's brown or
   black wire goes there.
3. **Terminal block.** Solder the blue two-way terminal block (V+ and GND).
4. **Capacitor.** Solder the capacitor into C2, minding polarity: the stripe (−) goes to the GND side, as marked on the
   silkscreen. Allow about 100 µF per servo; 1000 µF covers twelve DS3218s at idle and in gentle
   moves. Bend the leads 90° and lay the can flat on the shield, away from the servo headers: standing
   up, it's taller than the room under the top cover.

</Steps>

## Set the SZBK07 to 6.0 V

**Do this before any servo is connected.** A full 3S pack is 12.6 V, which would burn DS3218s (rated 4.8–6.8 V), and an unchecked
regulator can also burn the shield's capacitor.

<Steps>

1. Wire the bench supply (or LiPo) to the SZBK07's **IN+ / IN−** with 16 AWG. Check polarity with the meter.
2. Set the board's small **ON/OFF** slide switch, next to the IN terminals, to **ON**.
3. Switch on. With nothing on the output, measure **OUT+ / OUT−** with the multimeter.
4. Turn the **voltage (CV)** potentiometer until the meter reads **6.0 V**. It can take many turns.
5. If your module has a **current limit (CC)** pot, turn it to maximum for now.
6. Switch off.

</Steps>

<Aside type="caution" title="Output won't move?">
With nothing on the output, the CV pot moves the output by roughly a volt per turn. If it stays put however far you
turn it, check the meter on the supply itself, the input voltage at IN+ / IN− (at least 9 V), and the ON/OFF switch.
If those are all fine, the board is faulty: get another 300 W / 20 A "SZBK07" board, the same size, so it fits the
plate's posts. Until it arrives, the bench supply set to 6.0 V can feed the shield's V+ directly.
</Aside>

## Wire the bench rig

<PowerDiagram mode="bench" />

<Steps>

1. Press the shield onto the UNO Q.
2. Run 16 AWG from the SZBK07 **OUT+** to the shield's **V+** terminal, and **OUT−** to **GND**. The terminal block has
   reverse-polarity protection, but don't rely on it.
3. Connect the UNO Q to your computer with USB-C.

</Steps>

<Aside type="danger" title="Two separate supplies">
The servo rail (V+) is isolated from the UNO Q's VIN and 5V on the shield, and only the grounds are shared. Never
connect V+ to the UNO Q's 5V or VIN pins, and never take servo current from the UNO Q.
</Aside>
