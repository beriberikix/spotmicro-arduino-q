## Mechanical

| Problem | Fix |
| ------- | --- |
| Nuts fall out of their pockets while closing parts | A dab of Blu-Tack on each nut. Watch the two outer nuts by the upper-leg servo pocket in particular. |
| A bearing won't go in | Press, don't hammer: hammering cracks the part. Scrape or sand the seat lightly and press again. |
| A bearing is loose | Check the printer's X-Y hole compensation. As a last resort, a drop of CA glue on the outer race only, never the inside. |
| Leg doesn't fold flat, or there's a gap at rest | The horn is a tooth off. Refit the horn on its plate at a different arm until the plate sits square. |
| A joint can't reach its reference pose | Refit that horn one spline tooth over. |
| Upper leg wobbles on the hip | The shoulder cover's bearing isn't on its pin, or the cover screws are loose. |
| Cables won't pull through the top shell | Open the shells slightly; avoid sleeving. |
| A knee cable runs outside the upper leg | Route it along the outside, or take the leg apart to reroute it: see [Knee cable outside the upper leg](#knee-cable-outside-the-upper-leg). |
| Horn screws poke through and rub the servo | Thread an M2 nut onto each M2×8 as a spacer, or use M2×6. |
| A part snapped at a thin point | Reprint with more walls (3–4) and check the infill matches the [stl list](https://github.com/beriberikix/spotmicro-arduino-q/blob/main/stl/README.md). |

## Knee cable outside the upper leg

If a knee servo's extension cable wasn't laid in the top shell's groove when the upper leg was closed (see
[Upper leg](../../legs/upper-leg/), step 3), there are two ways to deal with it.

- **Option A, route it along the outside.** No disassembly and no calibration. Recommended.
- **Option B, reroute it through the upper leg.** Neater, but the top shell can't come off with the leg assembled:
  two of the hip-servo screws that hold it point straight at the servo cap, with only 8 mm behind their heads, and the
  cap itself can only come off once the shoulder servo is out of its mount. So the leg comes off the robot, and the hip
  and shoulder angles have to be set again afterwards. The knee keeps its calibration.

### Option A: along the outside

<Steps>

1. Unplug the battery.
2. Run the knee cable along the upper leg's inner face (the side facing the body), from the knee to the shoulder.
3. Hold it there in two or three places with small zip ties round the upper leg, adhesive cable clips or strips of
   tape. Keep it away from the knee joint's moving edges and from under the shoulder cover's rim.
4. Leave a slack loop where it crosses the knee and another where it crosses the hip, so neither joint pulls on it.
5. Reconnect the battery and move the knee, the hip and the shoulder through their range in servo_tool. Watch that the
   cable never goes tight or catches.

</Steps>

### Option B: through the upper leg

<Check id="knee-reroute-order" title="Option B's order is from the CAD">
This order comes from the CAD and the build pages, not from a leg taken apart. In particular, check that with the
shoulder servo out of its mount a hex key reaches the hip horn's centre screw.
</Check>

**Take it apart**

<Steps>

1. Unplug the battery, unplug the leg's three leads from the shield and pull them back out of the body.
2. Take the leg off the body, reversing [Shoulders into the frame](../../body/install-shoulders/). The shoulder servo's
   shaft comes out of its horn on the outer shoulder plate.
3. Take off the shoulder cover: its three <kbd class="hw">M3×20</kbd> from the cap side, and its bearing off the pin on
   the top shell. Keep the three nuts from the cover's slots.
4. Take the shoulder servo out of its mount: its four <kbd class="hw">M3×20</kbd> (reversing
   [Shoulder servo](../../shoulders/shoulder-servo/)). This clears the way to the hip servo's centre screw.
5. Undo the <kbd class="hw">M3×8</kbd> in the centre of the hip horn and pull the shoulder joint (mount, cap and horn
   together) off the hip servo's shaft. Leave the horn screwed to the cap.
6. Support the lower leg and undo the four <kbd class="hw">M3×20</kbd> that hold the knee plates to the upper leg, two on
   each side. The two on the servo-horn plate's side go into nuts in the top shell; the two on the bearing plate's side
   go into nuts in the bottom shell, so tape over those pockets. Leave both knee plates on the knee servo's horn and the
   bearing: the knee keeps its angle.
7. Of the four <kbd class="hw">M3×20</kbd> through the hip servo, undo the two that also pass through the top shell (now
   reachable); the other two hold the servo in the bottom shell. Hold or tape the bottom shell's outer nuts.
8. Lift the top shell off outwards.

</Steps>

**Route the cable**

<Steps>

1. Lay the knee extension cable straight and snug in the groove along the top shell's edge, from the knee end, and take
   its plug out through the hole at the hip end. A loop inside the groove gets pinched when the shells close.
2. If the hip lead doesn't come out of that hole already, put it through now too, one plug at a time.
3. Leave the slack at the knee end, where the cable goes in between the shells. With the top shell held on, move the
   knee by hand to fully folded and fully straight: the cable shouldn't go tight or come out of the groove.

</Steps>

	<figcaption>In the top shell: in between the shells at the knee end, along the groove, out of the hole at the hip end.</figcaption>

**Put it back**

<Steps>

1. Lower the top shell back on, both leads coming out of the hole and the knee cable in its groove. Check the cable
   isn't pinched along the shells' edges.
2. Put the two hip-servo <kbd class="hw">M3×20</kbd> back into their nuts, then the four knee-plate
   <kbd class="hw">M3×20</kbd>. The knee plates only fit one way, so the knee angle is unchanged.
3. Fit the shoulder joint back onto the hip servo and set the hip angle again: [Hip joint](../../shoulders/hip/),
   step 4.
4. Put the shoulder servo back in its mount ([Shoulder servo](../../shoulders/shoulder-servo/)).
5. Put the hip and knee leads through the slot in the cap's extension, leaving a loop for the hip, and fit the shoulder
   cover ([Shoulder cover](../../shoulders/cover/), steps 2 to 5).
6. Put the leg back on the body and set the shoulder angle again
   ([Shoulders into the frame](../../body/install-shoulders/)).
7. Plug the leads back into the shield, reconnect the battery and move all three joints through their range in
   servo_tool. Watch that no lead pulls or catches at the knee, the cap or the shoulder.

</Steps>

## Servos and power

| Problem | Fix |
| ------- | --- |
| A servo stutters or buzzes while holding still | It's pushing against a mechanical stop: check its horn alignment. If it does it on the bench with no load, replace it. |
| A servo turns ¾ of a turn for `s ch 0` → `s ch 180` | It's a 270° servo. Replace it with a 180° one. |
| A servo cuts out after a few seconds of pushing | DS3218 overload protection at stall. Normal standing and walking don't trigger it. |
| Servo got hot or died | Check the SZBK07 output is 6.0 V with a meter, and look for plastic blocking the joint: a stalled servo blocked by a part burns out. |
| `PCA9685 not found at 0x40` | Shield not seated, VCC select not soldered to 3V (VCC floating), or a solder bridge on the address pads. |
| Servos twitch when another moves | Add or enlarge the capacitor on C2 (Adafruit: about 100 µF per servo); shorten the SZBK07 → V+ lead. |
| UNO Q resets when servos move | VIN dipping: check the battery charge, and that the UNO Q's VIN lead is taken before the SZBK07, not from the servo rail. |
| Nothing in the Serial Monitor | Use App Lab's **Console → Serial Monitor** while the app is running: servo_tool prints through the Bridge's `Monitor`, not `Serial` (the D0/D1 pins). |
